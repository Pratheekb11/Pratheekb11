The Bureau investigates forgetting. Machines that forget what they did.
People who were never told how the machine works. Billboards nobody
remembers renting. Four cases are currently open.

---

### CASE 001 · NENAPU
**Subject:** an agent that edited my repo and left no witnesses.
**Status:** OPEN. **Evidence:** https://github.com/Pratheekb11/Nenapu

Two weeks after the fact I would open a pull request and find changes I
could not account for. Sandboxes made it worse: eight gigabytes of hard-won
context, and the next sandbox inherited none of it. Every session began
with amnesia and ended with a stranger's diff.

Other outfits claim to have solved this. I could not verify that any of
them stayed on my machine, and I do not hand over my transcripts on faith.
So the Bureau built its own: FTS5 retrieval, deliberate decay, distillation,
self-validation. The file gets *smaller* and *truer* over time, which is the
opposite of what an archive normally does.

Not production grade. In daily use regardless. Licensed MIT, take it.

### CASE 002 · AI-IN-FIVE
**Subject:** a public handed a black box and told it was magic.
**Status:** SHIPPED, still under surveillance. **Evidence:** https://ai-in-five.vercel.app

Everyone kept asking me how to learn AI, and everything I could point them
at was welded to whichever tool was trending that week. So: six chapters,
three modules on using it at work, six optional deep dives, one playable
game each. Feynman's rule enforced. If it does not survive plain language,
it is not finished.

**Field note, launch day.** 82 views. Almost nobody survived chapter one.
The chapters were single unbroken scrolls and readers were quietly drowning
in them. Cut each into short pages. Drop-off collapsed, people started
finishing, and the traffic went international. Roughly 300 views and 20
returning readers since. The autopsy taught me more than the build did.

No signup. No fear mongering. Not monetised. Not for sale.
